Customer Value · SAFE → TRUST

SAFE controls create TRUST.

SAFE defines the conditions for a trustworthy state change. TRUST names the customer and delivery outcomes those controls are meant to improve.

SSpecificationDefine the target, scope, state, and completion condition.
AAuthorizationEstablish ownership, permission, and fail-closed boundaries.
FFactsBind claims to sources, fresh checks, and readback.
EError-recoveryPreserve checkpoints, rollback paths, and safe resumption.
TRUST value domains

Controls matter only when outcomes improve

Quality and confidence

Specification and facts reduce false completion, incorrect decisions, and repeated work.

Risk and authority

Explicit permission and fresh evidence limit blast radius and make responsibility visible.

Speed with control

Known paths can proceed quickly while ambiguity and high-risk actions stop at the right owner.

Continuity and traceability

Checkpoints, receipts, and recovery preserve work across sessions and support audit at scale.

Evidence relationship

Value claims inherit the evidence ceiling

Process evidence

A green test can prove a verified artifact. It does not by itself prove runtime activation, publication, or customer outcome.

Outcome evidence

Customer value requires observed results attributable to the controlled workflow, not only a plausible mechanism.

Boundary: SAFE is a control model, not a lifecycle stage or a claim that every production environment currently enforces these controls.
